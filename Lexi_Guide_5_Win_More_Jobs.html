How to Win More Jobs
With Better Quotes
7 habits that separate tradespeople who win from those who wonder why not

Quoting isn't just about price. Two tradespeople can quote the same job for the same amount and one wins it and one doesn't. The difference is nearly always the quote itself — and the behaviour around it. Here's what actually moves the needle.

1
Speed wins — send the quote the same day
Customers often book the first quote that arrives. Not the cheapest. The first. Get your quote out within a few hours of the visit and you are already ahead of most of the competition.
First in often wins — even at a higher price
2
Look more professional than the competition
A branded, itemised, well-laid-out quote beats a WhatsApp message with a number every single time. Customers use the quote as a proxy for the quality of your work. If the quote looks good, they assume the job will too.
3
Show you understood the job
Reference something specific from your site visit. "Following my visit on Tuesday, I've noted the existing timber is in good condition and won't need replacing." It proves you were listening and builds trust.
This is what Lexi's quote description field is for
4
Make it easy to say yes
Include a clear call to action. "To go ahead, simply reply to this email." Don't make them figure out the next step. Friction kills conversions.
5
Follow up — every time, without fail
If you've sent a quote and heard nothing after 3-4 days, follow up. A short, polite message costs nothing and wins jobs regularly. Most tradespeople don't bother. That's their loss and your opportunity.
A follow-up is not being pushy — it's being professional
6
Collect reviews after every job
A Google review is worth more than any leaflet. After every completed job, ask. "If you're happy with the work, a quick Google review would mean the world to a small business like mine." Most satisfied customers are happy to help — they just don't think to do it unless asked.
7
Price for profit, not just to win
Winning a job at a price that makes you no money is worse than not winning it. Know your costs, know your time, and price accordingly. Busy and broke is not a business strategy. Sustainable pricing keeps you in business long term.
A quote you win but regret is not a good quote
